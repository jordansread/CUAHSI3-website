## How papers were ranked

Citations per year: total citations from OpenAlex divided by the years from the publication year to 2026 inclusive (so 2026 counts as a full year, which lowers the rate of recent papers). Of the 1,770 papers in the list, 1,276 have a DOI and 1,265 of those were matched by OpenAlex and can be ranked; the other 505 (494 with no DOI, mostly archive pages, and 11 with a DOI OpenAlex did not match) cannot. Usage figures for the 1,097 DOIs harvested on 30 October 2026 were reused; for the 181 DOIs added since (the CZNet and Shale Hills batches) OpenAlex was asked again on 31 October 2026 and matched 176. A paper counts once, under the first observatory it is tagged to.

Observatory lane: the two highest-ranked papers whose first observatory tag is that observatory (ten observatories, 20 papers).

Cluster lane: **no paper in the list carries a CZNet cluster tag** (cluster tags are on datasets only). So a paper is assigned to a cluster through the NSF award it names: the registry titles of 44 CZNet-era awards name their cluster ("Network Cluster: Dust in the Critical Zone...", "...Bedrock controls on the deep critical zone..."), and the Hub awards are 2012893, 2012593 and 2012748. **That mapping from award to cluster is my inference from the award titles, not a record in the data.** One paper per cluster and the Hub, never one already chosen for an observatory (10 papers).

## What to check before anything is marked

1. **The cluster papers are weak highlights by usage.** Their per-year rates are 0.5 to 24. The alternative is to leave the cluster lane out until the papers have had time to be cited. Jordan's call.
2. **The award-to-cluster mapping is mine.** Drylands is matched through one award only (2012475). The Big Data paper (10.1029/2022gb007351) names two awards, 2012123 (Big Data) and 2012669, whose registry title is the Dynamic Water cluster's, so it could stand for either cluster; the other nine each name awards of one cluster only (checked in the registry). Anyone who knows the clusters should confirm the ten papers belong to the cluster named.
3. **Broad papers are in** (for example the Anthropocene paper under Calhoun, tree mortality under Catalina-Jemez), as Jordan chose.
4. **No paper here is a preprint, and none was close.** Of the 1,265 ranked papers, 6 look like preprints (ESSOAr or EGUsphere DOIs, or an OpenAlex type of preprint); their best rate is 0.2 citations a year, so none would have made any lane. (A preprint is ranked only where no final paper exists.)
5. Dataset #6 has a later version in the list: the same title ending "2019-2023" (DOI 10.6073/pasta/09605b9c54b3fb809f78043f7872fe00, published 2023). The 2019-2022 version is the one with the 5 citations; the later version is not in the top 10. If the later version is the one to show, say so.
6. The "Observatory or cluster" column for datasets shows the record's tags other than the cross-CZO "national" tag. The Reynolds Creek flux-tower dataset (#10) has no site tag at all in the file, so it reads "No observatory recorded".
7. The "since 2019" caveat for datasets goes on the page that shows them.
