## The ten

### boulder (Boulder Creek)
- **summary:** Studies how erosion and weathering control the architecture and evolution of the Critical Zone, with a focus on slope, climate, ecosystems and rock properties.
- **body:** The observatory also followed the effects of the 2010 Fourmile Canyon Fire, which burned 26 square kilometres and changed ecosystems and the delivery of water and sediment downstream. It ran outreach programs from kindergarten to graduate fellows and the general public.

### calhoun
- **summary:** A landscape heavily degraded by over a century of farming, used to study how people and the land act on each other across space and time.
- **body:** Decades of detailed soil and above-ground biomass studies in the pine ecosystems of the southeastern U.S. have added much to what is known about their biogeochemical cycles.

### catalina-jemez
- **summary:** Measures the geomorphic, hydrologic and biogeochemical interactions that drive theory and models of how the Critical Zone evolves, in the arid and semi-arid landscapes of the U.S. Southwest.
- **body:** Arrays of sensors and samplers measure the fluxes and fate of water, carbon and lithogenic elements. A stated aim was to set up natural laboratories for the wider earth-science community and to help students and the public understand how Critical Zone processes connect.

### christina (Christina River Basin)
- **summary:** Brings together knowledge of water, mineral and carbon cycles to measure the human effect on carbon sequestration in the Critical Zone, from soils to the sea.
- **body:** The Christina River Basin, in Delaware and Pennsylvania, includes the Brandywine, White Clay and Red Clay Creeks, has a long agricultural and early industrial history, and is described by the observatory as among the best-studied watersheds in the nation. About half a million people rely on it for drinking water, agriculture and recreation.

### eel (Eel River)
- **summary:** Located along the Eel River in Northern California, it studies how Critical Zone "currencies" change, interact and propagate.
- **body:** Its field sites are Rivendell, the Angelo Coast Range Reserve and the Eel River watershed. The team spans four departments at the University of California, Berkeley.

### iml (Intensively Managed Landscapes)
- **summary:** Studies landscapes that people have changed intensively: they support economic prosperity, but the same change can degrade the environment.
- **body:** Three basins: Clear Creek in Iowa (intensive agriculture, artificial drainage, wetland loss and urbanization); the Sangamon River Basin in Illinois (farmed since the 1800s, when tallgrass prairie was converted, with changed drainage altering hydrology and biogeochemistry); and the Minnesota River Basin (among the most rapidly incising rivers in the world).

### luquillo
- **summary:** Studies how Critical Zone processes and water balances differ between landscapes with contrasting bedrock but similar climate and environmental history.
- **body:** The observatory is in Puerto Rico.

### reynolds (Reynolds Creek)
- **summary:** Builds an observatory in Idaho for observing, experimenting on and modeling soil carbon at the scale of an intermediate watershed.
- **body:** It is sited in the USDA-ARS Reynolds Creek Experimental Watershed. Its questions include how climate, vegetation, topography and the properties of rock and regolith control the distribution of soil carbon, and how land management, such as prescribed fire, and climate change will affect carbon storage.

### shale-hills (Susquehanna Shale Hills)
- **summary:** Emphasizes quantitative prediction of how the Critical Zone is created and structured, following the pathways and rates of water, solutes and sediment.
- **body:** One team, in one observatory in Pennsylvania, measures and models environmental fluxes.

### sierra (Southern Sierra)
- **summary:** A platform and program for investigating how the water cycle drives Critical Zone processes, with a focus on water balance and nutrient cycling.
- **body:** Its sites include Providence Creek (four instrumented headwater catchments at the rain-snow transition, 1700 to 2000 metres, in mixed-conifer forest) and Wolverton Basin in Sequoia National Park. The archive's note to colleagues says that after the CZO program ended in November 2020, measurements at the main Providence site continue under the USFS Pacific Southwest Research Station, and those at the San Joaquin Experimental Range and Soaproot Saddle continue under NEON.
