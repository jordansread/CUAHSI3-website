# Draft: a deeper audit of www.cuahsi.org against this site

Draft for Jordan, 6 November 2026. Nothing here is started and no issue is open. It follows the method of the Critical Zone sweep (several routes into the same facts, provenance on every record, controls, counts with denominators, each batch shown before it is applied) and builds on the October parity analysis (`agent/parity.md`, reports `261005_parity-stage0.md` to `261006_parity-stage7-status.md`).

## 4. What a deeper audit adds to October's

October asked "which bodies of work are missing". Before cut-over we also need to know whether what is here is **right**, whether every old address **lands somewhere sensible**, and whether the **new site's own surface works**. Seven workstreams. Each produces a report and a script that can be re-run, and none changes `content/` or `pages/`.

**W1. Re-crawl and diff the legacy site.**
New snapshot under a new date, from the sitemap and the navigation and the footer; compare with the 5 October snapshot. Report: URLs added, removed, changed (by a hash of the main text), and URLs found by only one route. Tooling: `scripts/parity/fetch.mjs` and `parse.mjs` exist. *Verify:* the crawl's count against the sitemap's 687 and October's 767; fetch three added and three removed URLs by hand.

**W2. Cut-over simulation: every legacy URL against the new site.**
Serve the built site with `public/_redirects` applied, then request each legacy path (all URLs from W1, plus every URL in the cited-URL list and the Search Console export). For each: final status, final route, hops. Output a table of legacy URLs that would 404, land on a generic page, or take more than one hop. *Verify:* the same run on the October list, to compare with the claim in `261006_parity-stage7-status.md` that no redirect existed for finished bodies; then three URLs by hand in a browser.

**W3. Item-level parity for items with identity.**
People (91 legacy pages), events (118), news (49), newsletters (19), programs, graduate programs (107 URLs, 102 institutions here), portals (44), documents (59 hosted), jobs, workshops. Match by stable keys (name, YouTube id, slug, external URL) and compare **facts a reader acts on**: names and roles, dates and status, counts, URLs, whether contact details differ (reported as "differ", never copied, P8). Discrepancies are listed with both locations and not resolved (P4). Matching uses the confidence scale of October (`exact`, `probable`, `weak`, `none`) and is never raised to improve a number. *Verify:* a controls set of 30 known pairs by hand; a subagent comparison on the same 30 and the number of verdicts that differ.

**W4. Links, files and embeds.**
Every outward link on this site (its own pages and content) checked for status, redirect and a dead-domain list, one request per host per second; every file the legacy site hosts (76 files in October) against what is hosted here and what the legacy page said; every embed (YouTube, forms, maps) on legacy pages with a counterpart or none. *Verify:* three dead links and three live ones opened by hand; HEAD results re-run on a sample.

**W5. Functions a visitor can do.**
Search, subscribe, donate, contact, join or renew, register for an event, post or apply for a job, log in, download a document, filter a list, watch a recording. For each: present, present differently, or absent, tested without submitting anything (a form is not submitted without Jordan's yes; payment and login are not tested). Output a table with the evidence for each row. October's plan listed it as a stage 3 comparison; I have not checked whether that table was produced.

**W6. Readiness for the new address.**
Titles and descriptions unique per page; canonical links; the sitemap (see section 3); robots and indexing; the 404 page; redirect chains; the Search Console steps for a move; social previews; structured data where it fits; accessibility on the 12 most visited pages with the October audit as the baseline (`agent/reports/261004_accessibility-audit.md`); page weight and load on the heaviest pages. Several items change `nuxt.config.ts`, the site address or indexing (rule 8): they are listed as proposals and none is made.

**W7. Cut-over rehearsal and checklist.**
The order of operations for the switch: who owns DNS, the hosts that must redirect (cuahsi.org apex and www, legacy sub-hosts), the date the legacy site goes read-only, a rollback, and a dry run on a staging name. The Critical Zone tickets #81 (host redirects and domain setup), #82 (retirement checklist) and #84 (go-live gate) cover the same mechanism for one section; this workstream makes them part of one site-wide plan so the two do not diverge.

## 5. How the sweep is run

- **Several independent routes into the same facts, with a provenance field on each record**, as in the Critical Zone harvest: the sitemap, the navigation, links found inside pages, Search Console URLs, cited URLs in newsletters and content. A URL one route finds and the others miss is listed.
- **Controls first**: for each script, a case whose answer is known, run before the full pass (for example the three finished bodies and their 273 URLs).
- **Counts with denominators and a sample read by hand** in every report: three items the script called matched and three it called missing (rule 12).
- **Subagents compare pages in parallel** (`page-comparer`, `reviewer`). Their findings are model output and are checked by a person before they go in a report; the October pilot's gate (stop if more than one of five verdicts differs) stays.
- **Read-only on both sites.** Reports and scripts go on `task/` branches. Anything that changes a page, a redirect or content is a separate PR on its own branch kind (rule 3), proposed from the report and approved first. Nothing is invented: a missing fact is listed as missing (C3).
- **Polite crawling.** A User-Agent naming CUAHSI and a contact address, one request at a time, at least 2 seconds apart on `www.cuahsi.org`, no logins, no form submissions, each response saved once under `raw/legacy-site/YYMMDD/` (gitignored) and every later step read from the snapshot.

## 6. Cost and size (my estimate, not a measurement)

Fetching about 770 pages at 2 seconds a request takes about 26 minutes; the link check and W2 run in minutes each. The time is in reading: W3 (item-level) is the largest, and it is where subagents help and where your review of discrepancy lists is needed. Proposed pull requests: about one per workstream for scripts and reports, then content or code fixes in as many PRs as the findings need. I would not start fixes until the ranked gap list is approved.
