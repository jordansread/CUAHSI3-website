The CUAHSI Virtual University (CVU) is a multi-institution, one-semester graduate program consisting of a diverse set of 4-week modules on specialized hydrology topics. The CVU enhances the depth and breadth of graduate course offerings at universities across the nation and facilitates networking among the hydrologic community.

## How it works

The CVU runs from September through early December each year. Students may select up to three modules from a list of specialized online hydrology topics, each taught by leading faculty from institutions across the country. Modules are offered synchronously twice per week (90 minutes per session) over four consecutive weeks.

Each module is equivalent to one-third of a semester-long course and includes evaluation elements such as problem sets, projects, presentations, and exams. The instructor at the student's home university assigns a final grade based on scores provided by the module instructor, and students receive regular course credit through their home institution.

Because the CVU is built on a reciprocal teaching model, only students from participating universities are eligible to enroll.

## For faculty

Faculty at U.S. universities are eligible to propose and teach CVU modules as part of their regular teaching responsibilities. CUAHSI solicits module proposals annually, with applications typically due in mid-March for the following fall.

**Faculty requirements:**
- Faculty member at a graduate program relevant to hydrologic sciences
- University consent to participate in the CVU
- Availability to teach synchronously for four consecutive weeks, twice weekly for 90 minutes
- Hold virtual office hours for students from other institutions
- Participate in at least one preparation call before the program starts

**CUAHSI particularly welcomes modules in:**
- Critical Zone Science
- Food, Energy, and Water Systems (FEWS)
- Remote Sensing
- Hydrogeophysics

## For students

Students at participating universities register through their institution's normal course registration process. Module topics vary by year and cover the breadth of hydrologic sciences. Students select modules based on their research interests and receive course credit at their home institution.

Students not at a participating university should speak with their advisor or faculty about encouraging their institution to join.

## Track record

Since its founding in 2017, the CVU has enrolled 430+ students from 25 institutions. Nine modules were offered in Fall 2025 across topics including snow hydrology modeling, ecohydrology of groundwater-dependent ecosystems, geospatial data processing with Google Earth Engine, and uncertainty quantification. A Data Competency talk on FAIR data practices and reproducible workflows was delivered across six of the nine modules in 2025.
