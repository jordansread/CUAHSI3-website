By its own account the observatory was established in 2007 around an 8-hectare forested catchment in central Pennsylvania, developed entirely on the Rose Hill shale. It asks how a catchment on shale bedrock evolves over time scales from minutes to millennia, and what controls its water and element budgets.

The archive says the Shale Hills watershed and a sister catchment, Leading Ridge, have been studied since 1958, and that the observatory builds on those records of streamflow, precipitation, climate and water quality. It describes seven satellite sites, the Shale Transect, that extend the work to shale elsewhere, including New York, Virginia, Tennessee, Alabama, Wales and Puerto Rico. It says its research team spans three colleges at Pennsylvania State University.
