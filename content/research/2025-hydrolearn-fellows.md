[HydroLearn](https://hydrolearn.org/) is an NSF-originated educational platform offering free, customizable active-learning resources in hydrology and water resources engineering. Through a series of three CIROH-funded projects, CUAHSI collaborated with HydroLearn to develop modules aligned with CIROH's research themes and to support the advancement of water prediction education.

In June 2024 and January 2025, virtual and in-person workshops brought together HydroLearn Fellows — faculty, students, and postdoctoral researchers — to build educational modules translating cutting-edge research into applied learning. Fellows were trained by both hydrologists and education experts throughout the module development process.

Fellows designed modules centered on real-world problems of practice across a wide range of water prediction topics:

- **Sushant Mehan and Ryan Wallace** — managing harmful algal blooms
- **Pawan Thapa** — climate change and culvert analysis
- **Ryan Johnson and Irene Garousi-Nejad** (CUAHSI) — hydroinformatics and snow modeling
- **Wouter Knoben and Diana Spieler** — adapted open-access teaching materials made more sustainable for instructors worldwide
- **Laura Rosales Lagarde and Rachel Housego** — hurricanes, groundwater, and floods

Despite their different topics, Fellows described a shared impact: HydroLearn lowers barriers for instructors, connects research to real-world problems, and keeps high-quality resources accessible across institutions regardless of faculty mobility. As Garousi-Nejad reflected, "Being a learner one day can naturally lead you to become a contributor the next."

CUAHSI and CIROH are planning another HydroLearn workshop. Explore the platform at [HydroLearn.org](https://hydrolearn.org/).
